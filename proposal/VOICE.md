# VitaminAir voice

Owner direction (2026-10-11): humble, heads down, truthful. Close to stoic. Talk about the work at hand, not about ourselves. No heroics, no AI-style writing, no marketing bluff.

## Stance
- **Small, deliberate work.** We do things that don't scale on purpose: one small plot, done carefully by hand, written down. If it works here, the notes may help someone elsewhere. Say that plainly, once. Never claim it will apply everywhere.
- **We are not saving anything.** We are not solving Cambodia's forests, the climate, or anyone's problems. We look after one piece of land and try to do it well.
- **Honest about what we don't know.** "We don't know yet" is a good sentence. Plans are plans, and the status labels carry the truth (TODAY / PLANNED TRIAL / LONG-TERM VISION).
- **Respect for neighbours.** The land is small for a carbon project but large next to an ordinary Cambodian farm. So we never use size to impress, and we never call it small to seem modest. State it once, plainly, where it's useful, and nowhere else.

## Facts and names
- **Location:** Kampong Seila, Cambodia.
- **Land:** "the land we look after" or "the land". Never "bought", "own", "acquired" or "our property". The size, "almost 100 hectares", appears once as a plain fact on the Rewilding page (an "About the land" row). It never appears in the hero, headings, page descriptions or social previews.
- **Partners:** KOOMPI, SmallWorld and StadiumX. List them as plain names ("Working with KOOMPI, SmallWorld and StadiumX."). No logos, and no descriptions of what each does until the owner confirms. Balance.eco and ISI remain unnamed (exploratory).
- **Tree names** follow `src/art/trees.ts`.

## How the writing sounds
- Short declarative sentences, mostly present tense for what is happening now and "we plan to" or "we want to" for what isn't.
- Concrete nouns: hectares, saplings, the dry season, hours of care, cost, survival. Say what we will measure, not what we hope to feel.
- One idea per sentence. Cut any sentence that doesn't add a fact, a plan or a limit.
- Plain words. Use "help", "plant", "watch", "write down", "look after".

## Never use
- **Hero or saviour language:** save, heal, restore the planet, change the world, make a difference, impact, mission-driven, champion, pioneer, transform, revolutionise, solution, game-changer.
- **Marketing filler:** passionate, innovative, sustainable future, ecosystem (as a buzzword), journey, empower, unlock, thrive, vibrant, holistic, synergy, world-class, unique, cutting-edge.
- **AI-writing tics:**
  - "not just X, but Y"
  - "it's not about X, it's about Y"
  - "In a world where…"
  - "Whether you're… or…"
  - rhetorical questions as openers
  - triplets in every sentence
  - colon reveals ("The result: …")
  - stacked em-dashes
  - "Here's the thing"
  - "Let's…"
  - "imagine…"
  - closing with an inspirational line
- **Exclamation marks, emoji, superlatives, and intensifiers** (truly, deeply, incredibly, vital, crucial).
- **Slogans dressed as sentences.** The one statement per page may be crisp, but it must still be literally true.

## Test before keeping a sentence
1. Is it true today, or clearly labelled as a plan?
2. Would a farmer next door read it as plain talk, not showing off?
3. Could a stranger check it, or does it at least not pretend to be checkable?
4. Would we say it out loud to someone visiting the land?

## Examples
- **No:** "We bought almost 100 hectares to restore Cambodia's forests and build a sustainable future."
- **Yes:** "We look after land in Kampong Seila, Cambodia. Most of it is recovering on its own, and we leave that alone. On a few cleared patches we want to test careful native planting and write down what happens."

- **No:** "Join us on our journey to heal the land!"
- **Yes:** "If you know native trees, nurseries or this part of Cambodia, we'd like to hear from you."
